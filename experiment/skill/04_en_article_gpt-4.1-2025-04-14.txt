Most of the mistakes that slow writers down are boring ones. Nobody fails because they picked the wrong font. They fail because they never finish, and there are about six ways that happens.

The first is polishing too early. I think this one does the most damage, because it feels like work. You fix the opening paragraph for the fifth time and the draft never gets past page two. Get it all down first, then fix it.

The second is not reading. Writers who stop reading run out of voices to learn from, and it shows. Read a lot, and read outside your usual shelf.

Third, waiting to feel inspired. That feeling doesn't keep a schedule. A little writing every day gets more done than a perfect free weekend that keeps getting moved.

Fourth, taking feedback as a verdict on yourself. Somebody saying chapter three drags is talking about chapter three. Keep what helps and drop the rest.

Fifth, skipping the plan (a full outline is overkill for most people, but a few lines about where the piece is going will save you from getting lost halfway through).

And sixth, playing it safe. If every piece is the same kind of piece, the writing stops getting better. Try a form or a topic that makes you a bit nervous.

None of this turns anyone into a great writer overnight. It just means more of the work gets finished, and only finished work can be fixed.
