The sound design fares no better; dialogue often sounds like it was recorded in a cavern, or abruptly switches from a whisper to a shout without cause. Footsteps appear to have been added by someone stomping around with oversized clown shoes, utterly out of sync with the visuals. As for the editing, scenes linger for agonizingly long stretches on mundane details before jarringly cutting to an unrelated shot of a doorknob. Continuity errors abound, with props mysteriously appearing and disappearing, and daylight shifting to night within the same conversation. The script, if one could even call it that, feels like a first draft dashed off on a napkin, replete with wooden exposition and attempts at clever banter that land with a thud. Even the background extras appear utterly bewildered, clearly wondering why they are there, a sentiment likely shared by any unfortunate viewer. This isn't just a bad movie; it's a profound exploration of cinematic incompetence, a dreary testament to how little effort can be expended while still technically completing a feature. One might derive more entertainment from watching paint dry, as at least the colors change. It’s a compelling argument for the immediate cessation of all independent film grants, or at the very least, mandatory basic film school attendance for everyone involved.
